# PART A — THE INDIAN BANKING SYSTEM

## 1. What does a commercial bank do?

A bank accepts eligible customer deposits, provides credit, facilitates payments, manages liquidity, and offers other regulated financial services.

**Example:** ABC Bank accepts ₹100 crore of customer deposits. It may deploy some of its resources in eligible loans and investments while maintaining statutory reserves, regulatory capital and adequate liquidity. It cannot simply lend all ₹100 crore because it must comply with banking regulations and prudential risk controls.

### Bank revenue versus profit

Suppose ABC Bank pays an average **6%** on ₹10 crore of deposits and earns **10%** on ₹10 crore of loans (highly simplified):

- Annual interest paid = ₹60 lakh.
- Annual interest earned = ₹100 lakh.
- Difference = **₹40 lakh**.

This represents a **simplified net interest income illustration**, not the bank's final profit. Salaries, technology, bad-debt provisions, funding mix, taxes and other expenses matter. The **interest spread** compares interest rates; **net interest margin (NIM)** normally relates net interest income to average earning assets, so it is not simply the difference between any two quoted rates.

**Exam trap:** RBI (central bank) and SBI (commercial bank) are not the same kind of institution.

## 2. Classification of banks in India

| Classification | Examples or meaning | Core distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| Public Sector Banks (PSBs) | SBI, Bank of Baroda, PNB | Public-sector ownership/control |
| Private Sector Banks | HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank | Privately held ownership structure |
| Foreign Banks | Banks headquartered abroad and licensed to operate in India | Foreign parent/head office |
| Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) | Uttarakhand Gramin Bank, Rajasthan Gramin Bank | Special statutory rural-banking mandate |
| Small Finance Banks (SFBs) | AU Small Finance Bank, Ujjivan SFB | Full lending capability, focus on underserved segments |
| Payments Banks | India Post Payments Bank | Payments and limited deposits; **no direct lending** |
| Co-operative Banks | Urban cooperative banks; StCBs and DCCBs | Cooperative ownership/structure |

**Remember:** These are useful institutional categories. A second, overlapping legal classification is **scheduled vs non-scheduled**.

**Memory point:** A large private bank does not become a PSB because the RBI regulates it. Ownership and regulation are separate issues.

### 2.2 Public vs private sector banks

Both may accept deposits, sanction loans and provide payment services under applicable RBI rules. Their main distinction is **ownership**, not whether they can perform ordinary commercial banking functions.

### 2.3 Small Finance Bank vs Payments Bank

| Feature | Small Finance Bank | Payments Bank |
|---|---|---|
| Accept eligible deposits | Yes | Yes, subject to restrictions |
| Directly make loans | **Yes** | **No** |
| Payments / remittances | Yes | Yes |
| Debit cards | Yes | Yes |
| Credit cards | Subject to applicable permission/rules | **Cannot issue credit cards** |
| Focus | Financial inclusion **and lending** | Payments and basic deposit access |
| Standard individual customer's end-of-day deposit balance ceiling | No special ₹2 lakh PB ceiling | **₹2 lakh** |

**Example:** A small shopkeeper may seek a business loan from an SFB; a Payments Bank cannot itself provide that direct loan. It may facilitate other services within applicable rules.

**Exam trap:** The Payments Bank ceiling refers to **end-of-day balance per individual customer**, not a universal lifetime deposit or transaction limit.

### 2.4 Scheduled vs Non-Scheduled Banks

A **Scheduled Bank** is included in the **Second Schedule to the RBI Act, 1934**. A non-scheduled bank is not included.

- Scheduled does **not** mean government-owned.
- SBI and many eligible private sector banks are scheduled banks.
- Do not confuse **Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs)** with the broader Scheduled Bank classification, which can also encompass scheduled cooperative banks.

## 3. Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) — highest-priority section

### Why RRBs were created

Many farmers, agricultural labourers, artisans and rural micro-businesses lacked affordable institutional credit. RRBs were established to extend banking and credit facilities to underserved rural areas and combine commercial-banking capability with a rural development focus.

**Key dates:**

- **2 October 1975:** First five RRBs began functioning.
- **Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976:** Core statutory foundation.
- **1 May 2025:** Fourth round of consolidation under the **One State–One RRB** approach took effect.
- **28 RRBs:** Number functioning after the 2025 consolidation (official DFS list checked for this revision).

**2025 merger fact:** RRB count declined from **43 to 28**. Do not memorize older counts from old mock books.

### 3.1 RRB ownership structure

Standard initially prescribed capital subscription in the RRB Act:

| Stakeholder | Share |
|---|---:|
| Central Government | **50%** |
| Sponsor Bank | **35%** |
| Concerned State Government | **15%** |

**Memory trick: Centre : Sponsor : State = 50 : 35 : 15.**

Legal nuance: The RRB Act also contains provisions for raising capital from other sources; the statutory 50:35:15 split is the exam's usual standard structure, not a claim that no other ownership arrangements can ever occur.

### 3.2 Sponsor Bank meaning

A sponsor bank is a designated bank that supports an RRB through such functions as managerial guidance, staff training, technology or operational assistance, alongside its equity participation.

**Examples verified from DFS current RRB list:**

- **Uttarakhand Gramin Bank:** Head office **Dehradun**, sponsor **SBI**.
- **Rajasthan Gramin Bank:** Head office **Jaipur**, sponsor **SBI**.
- **Uttar Pradesh Gramin Bank:** Head office **Lucknow**, sponsor **Bank of Baroda**.

**Exam trap:** An RRB is **not simply a branch of its sponsor bank**. It is a distinct statutory bank.

### 3.3 RBI vs NABARD for RRBs

- **RBI:** Banking **regulation** — prudential and other banking regulatory rules.
- **NABARD:** Statutory **supervision/inspection** of RRBs and specified rural cooperative banks; also rural development/refinance functions.
- **Sponsor Bank:** Managerial, technical, operational and equity support.

These functions may interact, but they are **not interchangeable**.

### 3.4 Practical RRB example

A farmer asks an RRB for ₹2 lakh to buy fertiliser, seeds and arrange irrigation. The RRB assesses eligibility and extends a suitable agricultural loan. NABARD may provide refinance to the RRB under eligible schemes; the farmer's immediate loan relationship remains with the lending RRB.

### 3.5 IT Officer connection: RRB merger and digital banking

During mergers, IT teams may have to handle:

- Core Banking System (CBS) migration and integration.
- Customer ID and duplicate-account mapping.
- Balances, loan accounts, interest schedules and reconciliation.
- UPI, NEFT, RTGS, AePS, debit cards and payment integrations.
- Data security, access management, business continuity and audit trails.

**Exam/interview connection:** A merger is not only a change of name; it is also a large banking-data and operational integration exercise.

## 4. Co-operative Banking System

Cooperative banks are part of a member-oriented cooperative framework. Their governance is different from ordinary shareholder-owned commercial banks.

### Rural short-term cooperative credit structure

```text
State Co-operative Bank (StCB) — state-level apex
               ↓
District Central Co-operative Bank (DCCB) — district level
               ↓
Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS) — grassroots/village level
```

- **StCB:** State-level cooperative credit institution.
- **DCCB:** District-level cooperative banking.
- **PACS:** Member-based primary agricultural credit societies; provide grassroots credit and other services.

**Very important:** PACS are **not automatically RBI-licensed banks**. They are outside the Banking Regulation Act's ordinary banking regulation; StCBs and DCCBs are regulated by RBI and subject to NABARD supervisory arrangements.

**RRB vs cooperative bank:** RRBs are separately constituted under the RRB Act, 1976; cooperative institutions follow the cooperative legal structure. They may serve similar rural customers but are **not the same category**.

## 5. Deposit Insurance — DICGC

**DICGC = Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation.** It is a wholly owned subsidiary of RBI and administers deposit insurance for eligible insured banks.

- **Maximum insurance:** **₹5 lakh per depositor per insured bank**.
- Limit includes **principal + interest**.
- Deposits held in the **same right and same capacity** are aggregated across branches of the same bank.
- Savings, current, fixed and recurring deposits may qualify, subject to specified exclusions.
- Deposits at **different insured banks** are generally considered separately for the limit.
- PACS deposits are **not automatically covered**, because a PACS is not itself an insured bank merely by being a credit society.

**Example:** ₹3 lakh in savings and ₹4 lakh in FD at the same insured bank, same ownership capacity = ₹7 lakh deposits, **maximum insured amount ₹5 lakh** (including interest within the applicable rules).

**Exam traps:** ₹5 lakh is not per branch, not per account and not a blanket guarantee for all financial investments.

# PART B — NABARD, PSL & AGRICULTURAL CREDIT

## 6. NABARD

**NABARD = National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development.**

| Fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| Established | **12 July 1982** |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Broad role | Apex rural and agricultural development financial institution |
| Refinance | Supports eligible rural lending institutions |
| Supervision | RRBs and specified rural cooperative banks |
| RIDF established | **1995–96** |
| SHG–Bank Linkage pilot | **1992** |

### 6.1 What does refinance mean?

Refinance is funding provided to an eligible lending institution against qualifying loans/activities, enabling the institution to support further credit.

**Example:** RRB has lent ₹100 crore to eligible rural borrowers. NABARD provides ₹60 crore in refinance under an assumed applicable scheme. NABARD's advance is to the **RRB**, not a replacement of the farmer's original RRB loan. The RRB must repay the refinance per its agreement.

**Do not confuse:**

- **Direct loan to farmer:** Usually from a bank such as RRB, commercial/cooperative bank.
- **Refinance to bank:** NABARD supports the lending institution.
- **RIDF:** Rural infrastructure funding instrument; not the same as the farmer's KCC.

### 6.2 Major NABARD functions

1. Refinance for agricultural and rural lending.
2. RRB and rural cooperative bank supervision/inspection.
3. Rural infrastructure support (including RIDF).
4. District credit planning through **Potential Linked Credit Plans (PLPs)**.
5. Financial inclusion and development initiatives.
6. Promotion of SHGs, JLGs and related rural credit mechanisms.

**Exam trap:** NABARD is **not India's central bank** and does **not determine the policy repo rate**.

## 7. Priority Sector Lending (PSL)

**Why PSL exists:** Credit may flow mainly to larger, well-established borrowers. RBI's PSL framework ensures qualifying lending to sectors with important economic or social needs, including agriculture, MSMEs, education and housing.

### 7.1 Eight PSL categories

1. Agriculture
2. Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)
3. Export Credit
4. Education
5. Housing
6. Social Infrastructure
7. Renewable Energy
8. Others (as defined under RBI rules)

Only **eligible** advances in each category qualify; not every loan with a similar name automatically qualifies.

### 7.2 Overall PSL targets — current framework

| Bank category | Overall target |
|---|---:|
| Domestic commercial banks (other than RRBs/SFBs), and foreign banks with ≥20 branches | **40%** |
| Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) | **75%** |
| Small Finance Banks (SFBs) | **60%** |

**Important update:** Older material often says SFB PSL = 75%. Under the current framework, **60%** applies.

### 7.3 What is the percentage calculated on?

The overall target uses the higher of:

- **ANBC:** Adjusted Net Bank Credit, or
- **CEOBSE:** Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposures.

Special rules and specified reference dates apply. It is **not simply a percentage of total customer deposits**.

**Example:** A qualifying RRB target base is ₹2,000 crore. Overall PSL obligation = ₹2,000 crore × 75% = **₹1,500 crore**.

### 7.4 RRB PSL sub-targets

| Category | Target / Sub-target |
|---|---:|
| **Total priority sector** | **75%** |
| Agriculture | **18%** |
| Non-Corporate Farmers (within agriculture) | **14%** |
| Small & Marginal Farmers (within NCF) | **10%** |
| Micro Enterprises | **7.5%** |
| Weaker Sections | **15%** |

**Nested target logic:**

```text
Agriculture (18% of applicable base)
   └─ Non-Corporate Farmers (14% of applicable base)
       └─ Small & Marginal Farmers (10% of applicable base)
```

Do **not** add 18 + 14 + 10. The 14% and 10% targets are subsets of agriculture-related lending, not extra mutually exclusive targets. Weaker-section classifications can overlap with qualifying priority-sector categories.

**Example (base ₹1,000 crore):**

- Overall PSL target = **₹750 crore**.
- Agriculture = **₹180 crore**.
- NCF sub-target = **₹140 crore**.
- SMF sub-target = **₹100 crore**.
- Micro enterprises = **₹75 crore**.
- Weaker sections = **₹150 crore**.

These targets **cannot** be summed to calculate total lending because of overlaps.

### 7.5 PSL shortfall

Banks falling short of applicable PSL targets can face prescribed remedies/contributions to funds such as **RIDF** and other designated funds, under RBI rules. A shortfall does not mean the loan book is automatically reclassified as NPA.

**Reference:** RBI PSL Directions, 2025, updated through **11 September 2026**.

## 8. Kisan Credit Card (KCC)

**KCC launched:** **August 1998**. Designed to provide timely, flexible and affordable institutional credit for agriculture and eligible allied activities.

### 8.1 How KCC works

A farmer receives a sanctioned credit limit after bank assessment and may withdraw amounts for eligible farming needs. Short-term KCC credit can be revolving, with repayment aligned to crop cycles. Investment/term-loan components may have different structures.

**Example:**

- Sanctioned credit limit: ₹2,00,000.
- Farmer withdraws ₹60,000 for seeds and labour.
- Unutilised limit: ₹1,40,000.
- Interest is generally charged based on **actual outstanding utilisation and time**, not automatically on the entire sanctioned ₹2,00,000.
- Timely repayment can restore revolving availability according to bank rules.

### 8.2 Current important KCC/MISS numbers (2025–26 announcements)

| Item | Exam-relevant figure | Precise interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Enhanced crop loan ceiling under Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)/KCC | **₹5 lakh** | Enhanced loan-limit announcement; **not** a guarantee of 4% interest over full ₹5 lakh |
| Collateral-free agricultural loan ceiling | **₹2 lakh** | General applicable collateral-security relaxation, subject to eligibility/rules; effective 1 January 2025 |
| Concessional 7% annual rate specified for eligible short-term agri loans | **Up to ₹3 lakh** | The relevant published subsidised rate applies to the specified ₹3 lakh limit |
| Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI) | **3 percentage points** | Eligible, timely repayment under scheme rules |
| Effective interest for eligible timely repayment | **4%** | 7% less 3 percentage points |
| Interest subvention to lending institution in FY 2025–26 scheme | **1.5%** | **Paid/support to lending institution**, not an extra 1.5% farmer discount |

**Warning:** The ₹5 lakh enhanced ceiling and the ₹3 lakh qualifying concessional-rate range are **different figures**. Follow current scheme-year circulars for any further changes.

### 8.3 Interest numerical

An eligible farmer uses ₹1,00,000 for one full year at 7% simple annual interest:

- Interest before prompt repayment incentive = ₹1,00,000 × 7% = **₹7,000**.
- If all conditions for 3% PRI are met: effective cost = ₹1,00,000 × 4% = **₹4,000**.
- Difference = **₹3,000**.

It is incorrect to calculate farmer's interest as **7 − 3 − 1.5 = 2.5%**; the 1.5% scheme benefit is for eligible lending institutions.

### 8.4 Who may be eligible?

Subject to bank/rules: owner cultivators, tenant farmers, oral lessees, sharecroppers, and eligible SHGs/JLGs of farmers. KCC also supports eligible allied activities such as animal husbandry, dairy and fisheries.

### 8.5 Collateral-free ≠ loan waiver

No separate collateral may be required within applicable limits; nevertheless, the borrower owes principal plus applicable interest. Loan underwriting, identification and repayment obligations still apply.

### 8.6 Common agriculture lending products

| Loan | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Crop loan | Seeds, fertilisers, labour, seasonal working capital |
| Farm mechanisation loan | Tractor, power tiller, implements |
| Irrigation loan | Pump sets, drip systems |
| Dairy / allied loan | Livestock and allied activities |
| Fisheries credit | Fisheries/aquaculture working capital |
| Agricultural infrastructure finance | Storage, cold-chain and related infrastructure |

# PART C — SHG, JLG, FINANCIAL INCLUSION & PMJDY

## 9. Financial Inclusion

Financial inclusion means providing usable, affordable, responsible and accessible formal financial services to excluded/underserved individuals and businesses.

Typical services: bank account, savings, payments, credit, insurance, pension and financial literacy.

**Example:** A farmer who relied on high-interest informal credit gains access to an RRB account, KCC, local BC-assisted transactions, and eligible insurance products.

### RBI Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index)

**Three dimensions:**

- **Access:** Can people reach formal financial services?
- **Usage:** Do people actually use these services?
- **Quality:** How appropriate/effective are the services?

Published index design weights: Access **35%**, Usage **45%**, Quality **20%**. For basic MCQs, most important is **Access–Usage–Quality**.

**Exam trap:** Opening an account improves access, but an inactive account may indicate weak usage.

## 10. SHG — Self Help Group

An **SHG** is a small, usually informal, member-based group that promotes regular savings, mutual support, internal lending and often livelihood development.

- Common size: **10–20 members**.
- Special DAY-NRLM groups may allow **5 members** in specified circumstances.
- SHGs are especially important for women's financial inclusion.
- NABARD started the pilot **SHG–Bank Linkage Programme (SHG-BLP) in 1992**.

### Example

12 members save ₹200 per person per month:

- Monthly group savings = 12 × ₹200 = **₹2,400**.
- In 6 months = **₹14,400**, assuming no withdrawals or earnings.

The group can make internal loans from its pooled savings. After demonstrating good records and behaviour, it may obtain a formal bank credit facility under applicable bank rules.

**Internal lending** = Group lends to its own member.  
**SHG bank linkage** = Eligible group accesses an account or loan through a formal bank.

---

## 11. JLG — Joint Liability Group

A **JLG** is normally an informal group of **4–10 people**, often small/marginal/tenant farmers, sharecroppers, oral lessees or similar borrowers, who access credit against mutual guarantees/joint liability arrangements.

- Joint liability is based on applicable signed agreements.
- Regular pooled savings are **not mandatory** in the same way as SHG practices.
- Bank may structure credit as **individual loans or group loans**, with mutual guarantee.

### Example

5 farmers need ₹50,000 each. Group-associated lending exposure = **₹2,50,000**. Since they have limited land collateral, the bank may accept the group mutual guarantee under a suitable loan product.

**Exam trap:** JLG is not simply a larger SHG, and a joint guarantee is not a loan waiver.

### SHG vs JLG

| Comparison | SHG | JLG |
|---|---|---|
| Typical members | 10–20 | 4–10 |
| Main purpose | Savings, internal credit, livelihood organisation | Mutual-guarantee-based formal credit |
| Pooled savings | Central practice | Voluntary/not defining requirement |
| Mutual/joint liability | Group mechanisms can exist | Defining lending feature |
| Typical beneficiaries | Rural low-income groups, often women | Small/tenant farmers, sharecroppers, micro-entrepreneurs |

**Memory trick:** SHG = **Save and Help Group** (memory aid only). JLG = **Joint Liability for Loans** (memory aid only).

## 12. DAY-NRLM

**DAY-NRLM = Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission**, under the Ministry of Rural Development. It aims to mobilise rural poor households into community institutions, promote sustainable livelihoods and strengthen financial inclusion through SHGs and their federations.

### Panchasutra — Five SHG practices

1. Regular meetings
2. Regular savings
3. Regular internal lending
4. Regular recoveries
5. Proper maintenance of books/accounts

**Exam trap:** A community revolving fund (where provided under programme rules) supports a group corpus; it should not automatically be confused with an ordinary bank loan. Different schemes and eligibility criteria apply.

## 13. PMJDY — Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana

**Launched:** **28 August 2014**.  
**Mission:** Expand financial inclusion with accessible basic accounts, payments, remittances, insurance, pension and credit linkages.

### PMJDY key features

| Feature | Important fact |
|---|---|
| Account type | Basic Savings Bank Deposit (BSBD) account for eligible unbanked persons |
| Minimum balance | **Zero** |
| Deposit interest | Applicable savings interest is earned |
| RuPay card | Available under the scheme, subject to issuance/activation rules |
| Accidental cover | **₹1 lakh** for earlier eligible PMJDY card arrangements; **₹2 lakh** for eligible new accounts opened after 28 August 2018, subject to conditions |
| Overdraft (OD) | Up to **₹10,000**, **only for eligible account holders** |
| DBT | Eligible accounts can receive government transfers |
| Insurance/pension linkages | PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, subject to independent eligibility/enrolment |

### Example: overdraft

Balance is ₹1,000; the customer needs to withdraw ₹5,000. If the bank has sanctioned a sufficient OD limit and permits it, the account can have a **₹4,000 borrowing/overdraft balance** after the withdrawal. Overdraft is **repayable debt**, not a gift or automatic entitlement.

### BSBDA facts

A Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account has **no minimum balance requirement**. Banks provide basic free services under RBI rules, including a minimum number of free withdrawals as prescribed. Additional withdrawals may depend on terms.

**Exam traps:** PMJDY does not guarantee an automatic ₹10,000 loan, and insurance claims are subject to policy conditions. A RuPay card alone is not a promise of unconditional compensation.

## 14. DBT — Direct Benefit Transfer

DBT means approved government benefits are transferred through authorised electronic payment mechanisms to eligible beneficiaries' accounts.

**Example:** An eligible beneficiary receives ₹2,000 directly in an account instead of through an intermediary cash-payment chain.

DBT is a **delivery mechanism** for benefits, not itself a bank loan, nor a guarantee that every account holder is eligible for every government scheme.

## 15. BC / Bank Mitra and AePS

### Business Correspondent (BC)

A bank may appoint an authorised **Business Correspondent** to provide last-mile assisted services such as account-related assistance, permitted cash transactions and other approved services.

**Example:** A village customer need not travel 20 km to a full branch for an eligible withdrawal if an authorised BC outlet is available.

### AePS — Aadhaar Enabled Payment System

AePS supports certain interoperable, Aadhaar-based banking transactions through the authorised banking network and appropriate authentication processes.

Simplified example:

```text
Customer → Authorised BC / micro-ATM → Authentication and bank checks
         → Approved transaction → Cash paid / account updated
```

**Security note for IT Officers:** Important implementation topics include biometric/authentication protection, consent and transaction authorisation, API integration, encryption, reconciliation, transaction idempotency, fraud monitoring and dispute handling. Aadhaar does **not** by itself authorise every debit.

# PART D — EXAM TRAPS, NUMERICALS & QUICK REVISION

## 16. Top 20 exam traps

1. **RBI ≠ SBI** — central bank vs commercial bank.
2. **PSB ≠ Scheduled Bank** — ownership vs RBI Act schedule.
3. **RRB ≠ sponsor bank's ordinary branch**.
4. **RRB regulator ≠ RRB supervisor** — RBI regulates; NABARD supervises.
5. **RRB ownership 50:35:15** — Centre : Sponsor : State.
6. **RRB 2025 count = 28**, not 43.
7. **SFB can lend; Payments Bank cannot lend directly.**
8. **Payments Bank ₹2 lakh limit** is normally the per-customer end-of-day balance ceiling.
9. **PACS ≠ automatically an RBI-licensed bank**.
10. **DICGC ₹5 lakh** is per depositor per insured bank, in same right/capacity.
11. **NABARD refinance ≠ farmer's direct loan**.
12. **PSL 75% for RRBs**, not 75% of all customer deposits.
13. **SFB PSL is 60%** under current directions, not outdated 75%.
14. **RRB agriculture 18%, NCF 14%, SMF 10%** — nested targets.
15. **Weaker-section and agriculture loans can overlap** in target classification.
16. **KCC sanctioned amount ≠ actual withdrawal**.
17. **KCC ₹5 lakh ceiling ≠ 4% effective interest over entire ₹5 lakh**.
18. **Interest subvention to bank (1.5%) ≠ additional discount for farmer**.
19. **Collateral-free ≠ repayment-free.**
20. **PMJDY OD ₹10,000 is eligibility-based, not automatic free money.**

## 17. Worked numericals

### Problem A — PSL target

RRB's applicable PSL base = ₹4,000 crore. Overall target = 75%.

**Solution:** 4,000 × 75/100 = **₹3,000 crore**.

### Problem B — Agriculture sub-target

RRB's applicable base = ₹2,500 crore. Agriculture target = 18%.

**Solution:** 2,500 × 18/100 = **₹450 crore**.

Within that same base, NCF 14% = **₹350 crore**, SMF 10% = **₹250 crore**. These are nested sub-targets, not separate additions to ₹450 crore.

### Problem C — SHG savings

15 members save ₹300 monthly for 8 months.

**Solution:** 15 × 300 × 8 = **₹36,000**, before any withdrawals/earnings.

### Problem D — KCC effective cost

Eligible farmer has ₹2,00,000 outstanding at 7% for one year. Assume full eligibility for 3% prompt-repayment incentive.

- Interest at 7% = **₹14,000**.
- Effective interest at 4% = **₹8,000**.
- Savings = **₹6,000**.

### Problem E — Deposit insurance

The same customer holds ₹2.7 lakh savings + ₹3.2 lakh FD with the same insured bank (same capacity).

- Aggregate = **₹5.9 lakh**.
- General DICGC insurance maximum = **₹5 lakh**, subject to applicable rules.

**Q1.** Scheduled banks are listed in which legislation?  
A. SEBI Act, 1992  
B. Second Schedule, RBI Act, 1934

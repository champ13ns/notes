## 1. The financial ecosystem: a simple map

India's financial system includes institutions that perform different jobs:

| Category | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Regulators | Set rules, supervise entities, protect users/markets | RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, IFSCA, IBBI |
| Development / specialised finance institutions | Provide, refinance, mobilise or catalyse long-term/sectoral finance | NABARD, SIDBI, NHB, EXIM Bank, NaBFID, IIFCL |
| Payment infrastructure | Build/operate interoperable payment rails | NPCI, NPCI Bharat BillPay Ltd (NBBL) |
| Financial safety net | Protect depositors against insured bank failure | DICGC |
| Coordination bodies | Coordinate regulators and financial-sector policy | FSDC |
| Financial intelligence | Analyse suspicious financial transaction information | FIU-IND |
| Market infrastructure | Clear/settle financial transactions | CCIL |
| Registries / guarantee institutions | Reduce collateral fraud or share credit risk | CERSAI, NCGTC, CGTMSE |

### The big four domestic regulators — memorize first

| Regulator | Regulates | Core memory |
|---|---|---|
| RBI | Banking, monetary policy, many NBFCs, payment systems, foreign exchange | **Banking + money + payments** |
| SEBI | Securities / capital markets | **Shares, securities, market intermediaries** |
| IRDAI | Insurance | **Insurance companies and policyholders** |
| PFRDA | Pension sector | **NPS and pension funds** |

**IFSCA** is the unified regulator for financial activities within an International Financial Services Centre (IFSC), such as GIFT IFSC. It is not the all-India regulator for ordinary domestic financial activity.

# PART A — DEVELOPMENT FINANCE INSTITUTIONS (DFIs / AIFIs)

## 2. RBI and All India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)

### RBI — Reserve Bank of India
- Established: **1 April 1935**
- Nationalised: **1 January 1949**
- Headquarters: **Mumbai**
- Main law: Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
- Other important legal frameworks include the Banking Regulation Act, 1949; Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007; and FEMA, 1999.

### Why RBI matters in this chapter
RBI is India's central bank and performs monetary, banking-regulatory, financial-stability, foreign-exchange, currency and payment-system roles. It also regulates and supervises several specialised financial institutions called AIFIs.

### RBI's core roles — quick recall
1. Monetary authority — formulates and implements monetary policy.
2. Banking regulator/supervisor — regulates banks and specified financial entities.
3. Currency issuer — issues banknotes (coins are minted by the Government of India and circulated by RBI).
4. Banker to Government — manages banking and debt-related functions for Central and State Governments.
5. Banker to banks — maintains bank accounts and provides central-bank facilities.
6. Foreign-exchange manager — administers India's forex framework under FEMA.
7. Payment-system regulator — authorises and regulates payment systems under the PSS Act.
8. Financial-stability and developmental role.

### All India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)
The key specialised AIFIs commonly tested in banking awareness are:

- NABARD
- SIDBI
- NHB
- EXIM Bank
- NaBFID

**Exam point:** RBI regulates and supervises these AIFIs under the relevant statutory framework and provisions of the RBI Act. They are specialised institutions; they are **not ordinary commercial banks**.

## 3. NABARD — National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **12 July 1982** |
| Law | NABARD Act, 1981 |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Ownership | Wholly owned by Government of India |
| Sector | Agriculture, rural development and rural credit |

### Why NABARD was created
It took over agricultural-credit functions of RBI and refinance functions of the former Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC). The aim was to strengthen institutional credit and support integrated rural development.

### Three major functions — C-D-S
**C — Credit**
- Refinance support to eligible rural financial institutions, including cooperative banks and RRBs.
- Supports short-term crop/seasonal credit and long-term investment credit for agriculture and allied activities.
- Supports rural infrastructure and other development-related credit.

**D — Development**
- Promotes agriculture, allied activities, rural livelihoods, institutions and financial inclusion.
- Supports Self-Help Group (SHG)-Bank Linkage and institution-building.
- Provides development assistance, training and capacity-building.

**S — Supervision**
- Plays an important supervisory/inspection role for Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) and rural cooperative banks within the statutory framework and in coordination with RBI.

### Important NABARD-linked facts
- **RIDF:** Rural Infrastructure Development Fund is associated with NABARD. It supports rural infrastructure projects, generally through financing to State Governments and eligible implementing entities.
- **SHG-Bank Linkage Programme:** NABARD is closely associated with scaling institutional linkage between Self-Help Groups and banks.
- NABARD is a development bank, not a retail bank where an individual normally opens a savings account.

### Exam trap
NABARD does not mean “only crop loans.” Its mandate covers agriculture, allied activities, rural infrastructure, rural institutions and broader rural development.

## 4. SIDBI — Small Industries Development Bank of India

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **2 April 1990** |
| Law | SIDBI Act, 1989 |
| Headquarters | **Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh** |
| Sector | MSMEs (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises) |

### Core mandate
SIDBI is the principal financial institution for the **promotion, financing and development** of the MSME sector and coordinates institutions engaged in similar work.

### How SIDBI supports MSMEs
1. **Indirect lending / refinance:** supports banks, NBFCs, MFIs and other eligible lenders that finance MSMEs.
2. **Direct lending:** fills selected MSME credit gaps through targeted lending.
3. **Promotion and development:** entrepreneurship, capacity building, credit-plus support and ecosystem development.
4. **Fund of Funds / ecosystem support:** supports startup and enterprise financing through relevant channels and programmes.

### Remember
- SIDBI = MSME.
- HQ = Lucknow.
- Established in 1990.
- It is not the regulator of all MSMEs or all banks; it is a development/financial institution.

## 5. NHB — National Housing Bank

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **9 July 1988** |
| Law | National Housing Bank Act, 1987 |
| Headquarters | **New Delhi** |
| Sector | Housing finance ecosystem |

### Main role
NHB is an apex development institution for the housing-finance ecosystem. Its work includes:
- Refinance and financial support to housing-finance institutions / eligible lenders.
- Promotion and development of a sound housing-finance system.
- Support for housing-related development and institutions.

### Very important regulatory change
Earlier, NHB had regulatory powers over Housing Finance Companies (HFCs), including registration.

**From 9 August 2019, regulatory powers over HFCs (including registration) were transferred to RBI.**

### Do not confuse
- **NHB:** housing-finance development / refinance institution.
- **RBI:** HFC regulator following the 2019 transfer.
- NHB is **not a retail home-loan lender for individuals** in the way a bank or housing finance company is.

## 7. NaBFID — National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Law | NaBFID Act, 2021 |
| Act came into force | **19 April 2021** |
| Established | **2021** |
| Commercial operations began | **29 December 2022** |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Sector | Long-term infrastructure finance |

### Why NaBFID exists
India needs long-term financing for infrastructure projects. NaBFID was created as a specialised Development Finance Institution (DFI) to address financing gaps and catalyse infrastructure investment.

### Functions
- Long-term financing / lending and investment for infrastructure.
- Mobilising institutional and private capital for infrastructure.
- Supporting development of infrastructure-finance markets, including debt/bond-market ecosystem.

### Exam trap
- NaBFID = infrastructure **DFI**.
- It is **not** a regulator.
- It is not a retail bank.
- The Act is 2021; operations began in December 2022.

## 8. IIFCL — India Infrastructure Finance Company Limited

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **2006** |
| Headquarters | **New Delhi** |
| Ownership | Government of India |
| Sector | Infrastructure finance |

### Role
IIFCL provides long-term financial assistance to viable infrastructure projects, including eligible projects under public-private partnership (PPP) structures.

It helps address the long tenor and large funding needs of infrastructure assets such as transport, energy, water and other eligible infrastructure.

### Distinguish IIFCL from NaBFID
Both are infrastructure-finance institutions, but:
- **NaBFID** is a statutory DFI created under the NaBFID Act, 2021 with a broad long-term infrastructure-finance and market-development mandate.
- **IIFCL** is a Government-owned infrastructure-finance company established in 2006.

Do not call either a financial regulator.

# PART B — FINANCIAL SAFETY NET AND PAYMENT INFRASTRUCTURE

## 11. DICGC — Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Original Deposit Insurance Corporation began | **1 January 1962** |
| DICGC formed | **15 July 1978** |
| Law | DICGC Act, 1961 |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Ownership | Wholly owned subsidiary of RBI |

### History
- Deposit Insurance Corporation (DIC) began in 1962.
- Credit Guarantee Corporation of India Ltd. (CGCI) was established in 1971.
- DIC and CGCI were merged on **15 July 1978**, creating DICGC.

### What does DICGC insure?
Eligible deposits such as:
- Savings deposits
- Current deposits
- Fixed deposits
- Recurring deposits

### Insurance limit — must memorise
**Maximum ₹5 lakh per depositor per insured bank**, including **principal + interest**, held in the **same right and same capacity**.

### How aggregation works
Suppose the same person has:
- Savings: ₹2 lakh
- FD: ₹2 lakh
- RD: ₹1.5 lakh
- Interest accrued: ₹20,000

At the same bank, in the same right and capacity, these balances are aggregated. The insured amount cannot exceed ₹5 lakh.

**Different branches of the same bank do not create separate ₹5 lakh limits.** The coverage is applied bank-wise, not branch-wise.

If the person deposits money in two different insured banks, the limit is applied separately for each bank.

### Who pays the insurance premium?
**The insured bank pays the premium to DICGC. The depositor does not pay a separate DICGC premium.**

### What is generally not insured?
Important exclusions include:
- Deposits of foreign governments
- Deposits of Central and State Governments
- Inter-bank deposits
- Deposits received outside India
- Other categories specifically excluded under the law

### Coverage includes
Eligible insured commercial banks and cooperative banks, including the applicable categories of RRBs, small finance banks and payments banks.

**Primary cooperative societies are not themselves insured banks.** Do not confuse a primary cooperative credit society with a cooperative bank.

### When can protection be triggered?
Coverage applies in the situations prescribed by law, such as bank liquidation and relevant resolution / merger situations. Amendments also provide a mechanism for time-bound interim payments to eligible depositors of banks placed under RBI's All Inclusive Directions (AID), subject to statutory conditions.

### DICGC exam traps
1. ₹5 lakh includes **interest**, not principal alone.
2. Coverage is **per depositor, per bank**, not per account or per branch.
3. Deposits in the same right and capacity are aggregated.
4. Insurance premium is paid by the **bank**.
5. DICGC is an RBI subsidiary, not a commercial bank.
6. A deposit insurance cover is not a guarantee that a bank can never fail; it protects eligible depositors within the statutory limit.

## 12. NPCI — National Payments Corporation of India

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established / incorporated | **2008** |
| Nature | Not-for-profit company (Section 8 company under current Companies Act framework) |
| Origin | Initiative of RBI and Indian Banks' Association (IBA) |
| Role | Umbrella organisation for retail payment systems |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |

### Why NPCI was created
To consolidate and build scalable, interoperable nationwide retail payment infrastructure, reducing fragmentation and making payments accessible, efficient and affordable.

### Major NPCI products / systems
| Product / system | Purpose |
|---|---|
| UPI | Instant interoperable bank-to-bank mobile payments |
| IMPS | Immediate interbank electronic funds transfer |
| RuPay | Domestic card payment network |
| NACH | Bulk recurring payments and collections |
| AePS | Aadhaar-enabled banking transactions |
| APBS | Aadhaar-based benefit/payment routing |
| BBPS / Bharat Connect | Interoperable bill-payment ecosystem |
| NETC / FASTag | Electronic toll collection |
| NFS | National Financial Switch / ATM network infrastructure |
| CTS | Cheque Truncation System infrastructure |

### Regulator vs operator — important
- **RBI:** regulates/authorises payment systems under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.
- **NPCI:** operates and develops major retail-payment infrastructure and schemes.

NPCI is **not the banking regulator**.

---

## 13. NBBL — NPCI Bharat BillPay Limited

NBBL is a wholly owned subsidiary of NPCI.

### Main role
It operates and develops the Bharat BillPay / Bharat Connect bill-payment ecosystem, enabling interoperable bill payments and collections across participating billers and payment channels.

### Examples of bill-payment categories
Electricity, water, gas, telecom, DTH, education, municipal payments and other eligible billers.

### Remember
- NPCI → umbrella retail payment organisation.
- NBBL → bill-payment ecosystem / Bharat BillPay / Bharat Connect.
- RBI → payment-system regulator.

# PART C — REGULATORS

## 14. SEBI — Securities and Exchange Board of India

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established as a non-statutory body | **12 April 1988** |
| Statutory status | **1992** |
| SEBI Act came into force | **30 January 1992** |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Sector | Securities / capital markets |

### Main objectives
1. Protect investors in securities.
2. Promote development of securities markets.
3. Regulate securities markets.

### What SEBI regulates
- Stock exchanges
- Brokers and other intermediaries
- Mutual funds
- Depositories and depository participants
- Merchant bankers
- Portfolio managers
- Investment advisers and other regulated market participants

### Example
If the issue involves listed shares, stock-market intermediaries, mutual funds or securities-market conduct, think **SEBI**.

### Trap
SEBI does not regulate ordinary banking deposits or monetary policy. Those are primarily RBI areas.

| Established / statutory authority | **2000** |
| Headquarters | **Hyderabad** |
| Sector | Insurance and reinsurance |

### Key responsibilities

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established as interim authority | **23 August 2003** |
| Statutory law | PFRDA Act, 2013 |
| Statutory framework effective | **2014** |
| Headquarters | **New Delhi** |
| Sector | Pension |

### Main role
Promotes old-age income security and regulates/develops the pension sector, including the National Pension System (NPS).

### Associated schemes / systems
- NPS
- Atal Pension Yojana (APY)

## 17. IFSCA — International Financial Services Centres Authority

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Law | IFSCA Act, 2019 |
| Established | **27 April 2020** |
| Headquarters | **GIFT City, Gandhinagar, Gujarat** |
| Jurisdiction | Financial activities in IFSCs in India |

### Why IFSCA was created
Before IFSCA, financial activity within an IFSC was regulated by the domestic regulators—RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA—according to their respective domains.

IFSCA was created as a **unified regulator** for development and regulation of financial products, financial services and financial institutions operating in India's IFSC.

### It covers IFSC activity across areas such as
- Banking
- Capital markets
- Insurance
- Pension
- Fund management
- Other permitted financial services

### Exam trap
IFSCA is a unified regulator **within IFSCs**, not a replacement for RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA across the whole domestic Indian financial system.

## 19. FSDC — Financial Stability and Development Council

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **30 December 2010** |
| Chairperson | **Union Finance Minister** |
| Nature | Non-statutory apex coordination council |
| Secretariat | Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance |

### Purpose
- Financial stability
- Financial-sector development
- Inter-regulatory coordination
- Financial inclusion
- Financial literacy
- Macroprudential / systemic-risk discussion

### Members / participants
The council includes key financial-sector regulators and senior Government officials. Commonly recalled members include RBI Governor and heads of SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA, along with senior Ministry of Finance officials and the Chief Economic Adviser.

## 20. FIU-IND — Financial Intelligence Unit–India

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **18 November 2004** |
| Administrative setting | Government of India / Ministry of Finance |
| Reports to | Economic Intelligence Council (EIC), headed by Finance Minister |
| Role | Financial intelligence and AML/CFT |

### Main functions
- Receives financial transaction reports from reporting entities.
- Processes and analyses information.
- Disseminates useful intelligence to appropriate law-enforcement, intelligence and regulatory authorities.
- Coordinates with domestic and foreign Financial Intelligence Units.
- Supports the national AML/CFT framework.

### Important report abbreviations
- **STR:** Suspicious Transaction Report
- **CTR:** Cash Transaction Report
- **CBWTR:** Cross-Border Wire Transfer Report
- Other prescribed reports include reports involving non-profit organisations and relevant transactions.

### Trap
FIU-IND is a **financial intelligence agency**. It receives, analyses and disseminates financial intelligence; it is not a bank regulator and does not itself replace investigative or prosecutorial agencies.

## 21. CCIL — Clearing Corporation of India Limited

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **2001** |
| Headquarters | **Mumbai** |
| Role | Clearing and settlement / market infrastructure |

### Main areas
CCIL provides clearing, settlement and risk-management infrastructure for eligible financial-market transactions, including Government securities, foreign exchange and money-market transactions.

### Concept
A clearing corporation helps manage counterparty and settlement risks by operating the clearing/settlement framework. CCIL is market infrastructure, **not a regulator**.

## 22. CERSAI — Central Registry

**Full form:** Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India.

### Purpose
Maintains a central registry of security interests created in favour of lenders, helping prevent fraud such as multiple financing against the same asset without disclosure.

### Banking relevance
- Central registration/search of security interests.
- Helps lenders identify existing charges.
- Supports transparency in secured lending and relevant SARFAESI processes.

### Trap
CERSAI is a registry / fraud-prevention infrastructure, not a bank and not the main banking regulator.

---

## 23. NCGTC — National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Limited

NCGTC is a credit-guarantee-related institution that acts as a trustee / administrator for various credit guarantee schemes.

### Why it matters
Credit guarantees can encourage lenders to provide credit to targeted segments by sharing part of the credit risk according to the scheme's rules.

### Remember
- Credit guarantee is **not the same as a loan**.
- A guarantee does not mean that every default is automatically covered in full.
- Scheme conditions, eligible lender/borrower, guarantee percentage and claim rules matter.

Do not confuse NCGTC with **CGTMSE**, which is a separate credit-guarantee trust framework associated with MSME credit guarantees.

---

## 24. Credit Information Companies (CICs)

Examples:
- TransUnion CIBIL
- Experian
- Equifax
- CRIF High Mark

### Role
Collect and maintain credit-related information from member lenders and produce credit reports / scores and related information products.

### Regulator
Credit Information Companies are regulated under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and RBI's regulatory framework.

### Trap
A credit bureau is **not a lender and not a regulator**. It provides credit information to help lenders assess risk.

4. EXIM Bank is headquartered in **Mumbai**.
5. NaBFID's Act is from **2021**; commercial operations started in **2022**.
6. DICGC's maximum insurance is **₹5 lakh including interest**.
7. DICGC insurance is per depositor per bank—not per branch or per account.
8. The bank pays the DICGC insurance premium, not the customer.
9. NPCI is the retail-payment infrastructure umbrella; RBI is the payment-system regulator.
10. NBBL is the NPCI subsidiary responsible for the Bharat BillPay / Bharat Connect ecosystem.
